SPACE & CRAFT

Space & Craft

Earthen walls, a single slab of hinoki and handmade chairs: twelve seats shaped by craftsmen.

01

Earthen walls and the kamado

Plasterwork: Kazuma Matsuki, Soukuchikusha (Yokkaichi, Mie)

The entrance wall and the kamado where we cook our rice are by Kazuma Matsuki of Soukuchikusha, a plastering workshop in Yokkaichi, Mie. The second generation of the workshop, he learned the traditional craft of earthen and lime plaster from his father, Kenji Matsuki, a nationally recognised master plasterer.

Mixed by hand and built up coat by coat with the trowel, the clay wall catches the light in soft shadows and changes with the seasonal branches set against it. The kamado is plasterwork too: the rice cooked in it closes every course.to confirm

02

The hinoki counter

Counter and furniture: BROOK (Yokkaichi, Mie)

The counter is a single slab of hinoki cypress by BROOK, a furniture maker in Yokkaichi, Mie, founded on a working sawmill. They take hinoki grown for around 80 years in the mountains of Owase, Mie, and handle every step themselves, from choosing the boards and milling to finishing in their own workshop.

Their aim is to keep the character of each individual tree in the finished piece. Sit close to the chef's hands, with the grain and scent of the wood in front of you; the wood will deepen in colour over the years, growing along with the restaurant.to confirm

03

The chairs

Chairs: AJIM / Kawabata Soushoku (Nagasaki)

The counter chairs are by AJIM, the furniture brand of Kawabata Soushoku, a Nagasaki workshop that has built seating for ships for half a century. The sturdiness and comfort that furniture at sea demands carry over to our counter.

A solid-wood frame with a softly padded fabric seat and back, chosen to sit with the hinoki counter and to stay comfortable through a long meal. Each chair is made to order in AJIM's own Nagasaki workshop.model / reason to confirm
